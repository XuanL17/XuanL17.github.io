Making Human Studies as Reproducible as Open-Source Code

Xuan LiuMarch 10, 2026—views
English中文

In machine learning, we’re used to picking up where someone else left off. Download a model and a dataset, run the public code, change something, and try an idea.

But when I read an interesting social science experiment and want to change the wording or a condition, I often have to reconstruct the materials, conditions, procedure, and analysis first. Running it with people again also involves ethics review and recruitment. A small change becomes a whole new project.

My advisor recommended David Donoho’s Data Science at the Singularity, which discusses frictionless reproducibility. My takeaway: a field can move quickly when one person’s work becomes the next person’s starting point. I want that for human studies too.

I want an experiment to be something we can share and run like a program, with the materials participants see, the tasks they complete, the procedure, and the statistical analysis all built in. Others can rerun it, change a condition for a pilot, or plug in different agents as participants. Each attempt keeps its materials, procedure, and analysis together, ready for the next person to build on.

That’s why I started building HumanStudy-Hub: working with a community to make human studies reusable and runnable.

These experiments can also be benchmarks for agents, or a gym: keep the experiment fixed, change the model, prompts, or participant settings, then compare with human results. See where the behavior matches, where it doesn’t, and keep adjusting. We also ran preliminary experiments with HumanStudy-Bench to see how changing the model, prompts, and participant settings affects whether agents reproduce human behavioral effects.
