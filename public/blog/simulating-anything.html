Simulating Anything,
as You Imagine It

Xuan LiuNovember 4, 2026—views
English中文

In my work on LLM simulation, I’ve noticed that when we talk about agents, we often picture a person first: someone who talks, makes decisions, remembers things, and has a personality.

When I picture an agent, I might see a dog, a room, or something that doesn’t exist in the real world.

Speaking of dogs, I asked two people: what is the dog you imagine like?

One said she wanted a little white dog, soft, with black eyes, about the size of a desk lamp. Then she added: preferably one that could talk.

The other began quite differently. Dogs poop, he said, and you have to pick it up. They guard their food. When you’re eating, they sit beside you begging for some. You talk to them, and they don’t necessarily listen. “I don’t even know whether it doesn’t understand or is pretending not to.”

Then I asked: you’re at home together, and there’s a sudden sound outside. What does the dog do?

She said she would be lying in bed. The dog would climb onto her, look out the window beside them to see where the sound came from, then return to her arms. Even better if it could tell her what was happening outside.

He said it would rush to the door, barking. If he got up and walked over too, it would bark even louder—getting bolder with its owner behind it. You keep saying, “Stop barking, stop barking,” and it just keeps going.

The agents I want to study could start from either description. They could begin with everyday experience, or with “I just want something like this.” A talking dog, an ordinary room, a room that gradually changes as visitors come and go—all could be things to explore.

We can imagine what it is, how it acts, and how it relates to its surroundings, then explore how LLMs might enact those ideas.

The believable agents I’m interested in belong in this space too. What “believable” means, and who gets to decide, is something I’d like to talk about in the next post.
